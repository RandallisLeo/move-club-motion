# Media / Generate

## Focus

An animation study with one Generate button and a vertical style selector. The loading surface is the subject.

- **Flow:** three monochrome regions are present together from the first frame, spreading across a dense stationary circle grid. Each region wanders through a different continuous noise field while independently expanding and contracting. Every region expands radially as a circle. Center distances and radius use CSS pixels on both axes, so the 3:2 frame never stretches them into ellipses; there is no taper, shear, or nonuniform scaling. All regions use a lighter grey, RGB 196, 196, 196, with size and opacity carrying the motion. Resting dots use RGB 220, 220, 220. Maximum region radii are about 40% larger than the first monochrome version, with the same contracted size, paths, timing, and breathing phases. The strongest center dots reach 4.8px in diameter, tapering continuously to the 2.04px resting size at the region boundary. Outside the bounded regions, dot size, opacity, and shade remain unchanged.
- **Sweep:** a broad, soft cool-grey wash crosses diagonally as a shallow arc, like a passing breeze. Its radius advances at a constant speed. Fine dots become clearer inside it and fade back to a faint resting texture behind it. The dot positions and sizes stay fixed; only their opacity follows the sweep, from 4% at rest to about 50% at the center. The wash has a soft trailing edge and no narrow leading stripe. It travels fully beyond the frame before each repeat, so the reset does not flash. The right-hand loading animation in the user-supplied video (around 19–23 seconds) informs its restrained tonal treatment. The reference is inspected locally, not bundled or published.
- **Glass:** a monochrome ripple study on a complete pale rounded card. Fine light-grey dots stay on a 5.6px grid while individual circular waves expand outward. The initial radial footprint remains 100px wide, capped at 65% of the shorter dimension on very small cards. A continuous rounded strength profile replaces the flat, full-strength core: dots become smaller and fainter toward both sides of the band, keeping its width without a solid-looking ring. Center dots initially measure about 3.1px across and shrink continuously as the band narrows. Opacity fades gradually from 20% to 90% of each wave's lifetime; a soft fade over the last 28px near each card edge prevents hard contact with the frame. Travel still extends beyond the corners, preserving circular expansion across the rectangular card. The dot color remains a soft grey, RGB 155, 159, 166. There are no colored layers, icon, or stage text. Glass remains selected initially.

The styles have different choreography. Sweep uses a 3.4-second constant-speed cycle, with its reset outside the frame. Its main wash is more than twice as broad as the earlier concentrated body, with peak opacity just under 6%; the dots provide the stronger visible texture. Flow has no sequential color entrances, scheduled painting passes, or repeating orbital path. Seeded cubic noise preserves continuous velocity, with separate horizontal and vertical rates for each region. Each radius changes by roughly 3.7 times, with independent phases and slowly varying breathing rhythms; even its smallest state retains a visible center. Glass emits one wave every 3.5 seconds. Each wave has a 5-second lifetime and a brief soft onset. Its radius releases more quickly at first and gradually slows, while thickness and intensity decay independently. The initial preview starts 0.45 seconds into the first wave; its footprint remains 100px wide through that moment, then narrows continuously. Circular distances use CSS pixels on both axes.

## Matrix reference and adaptation

The fourth option, **Matrix**, adds a compact centered 5×5 circle grid. The visual reference is [Neon Drift in Dot Matrix by shawn](https://dotmatrix.zzzzshawn.cloud/), inspected on September 16, 2026. The reference's enlarged preview used an approximately 1.36-second cycle, alternating diagonal phases, and stationary circular dots. Our independently written Canvas implementation uses a 3.8-second cycle and a continuous cosine pulse, with smooth rise and fall instead of linear brightness changes. The muted grey cells stay visible at rest and keep exactly the same size throughout; only opacity changes. This small fixed matrix has a distinct rhythm from the three existing full-frame fields. It uses the existing loading lifecycle, 60fps cap, reduced-motion still frame, and result reveal. No upstream component, dependency, or source is included. The upstream repository's custom license restricts redistribution as standalone reusable components; the reference is credited as visual inspiration, not offered as a vendored library component.

## Color direction

Flow, Glass, and Matrix are currently monochrome: grey dots over a pale card. Flow replaces its earlier RGB palette with equal red, green, and blue channel values, preserving its three independent moving regions. Glass has no colored background layers. The distinct movement of each study carries its character.

## Interaction

Generate starts an 8.5-second simulated wait. The borderless button changes to white with grey “Generating…” text while it runs. Changing the style starts a fresh preview immediately. Once ready, the image begins a 200ms fade over the last loading frame, with 2px of softness resolving to full focus. In Glass, the pale card and last ripple frame stay underneath the arriving image, preventing a flash. There is no additional wait or change in image scale. Reduced motion keeps the dot fields still and skips the arrival animation when the simulated wait completes. Screen readers receive loading, completion, and error announcements.

The result is an original image of translucent jelly flowers, made with the built-in imagegen tool. Its prompt is saved with the local JPEG asset in `public/media-generate/`. Each preview reuses that image and does not call a generation service. The wait is a demo timer, not a backend progress event. Timers are cleared when restarting, switching styles, completing, or unmounting.

The loading canvas and image share the image's 3:2 ratio. The preview fits both the available width and height, rather than stretching the frame and letterboxing the result. Replay clears the old timers and resets the study. Cached image readiness is checked after hydration.

## Rendering

Canvas 2D renders Flow up to 30 frames per second and Sweep and Glass up to 60, at a maximum device pixel ratio of 2. Dot spacing stays constant in screen pixels during resize. Sweep combines two broad tonal envelopes over a fixed surface gradient. Both the wash and texture use concentric radial gradients originating well beyond the upper-left corner, producing a shallow arc across the frame. Their radii are normalized between the nearest and farthest frame corners, preserving the complete exit before each repeat at every size. Its deterministic 32px texture tile contains fine 1.44–1.76px dots at roughly 4px intervals. The cached pattern is masked by a moving opacity gradient in a separate canvas and composited over the wash; individual dots never jitter or grow. Offscreen, background-tab, idle, and completed views do not redraw the canvas continuously. Glass draws its pale surface and grey dots in the canvas. Dot coordinates and circular distances are cached during resize. Each active wave has its own age, radius, band width, dot size, and opacity, replacing the former constant-width repeating wave and edge-only fade. Its previous CSS color layers and their animations have been removed. Reduced motion stops the dot animation while preserving loading announcements.

All local styling is scoped to `.media-generate-demo`. `.media-generate-card` provides only the gallery span; the shared `.demo-stage` remains the source of stage height.
